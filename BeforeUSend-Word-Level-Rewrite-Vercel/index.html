01
Separate the event from a judgment about the person.

If the draft says what someone “always” does, what they “never” understand, or what kind of person they are, replace that judgment with the specific behavior that is bothering you. Concrete events give the other person something they can actually respond to.

02
Describe the effect on you without claiming to know why the other person acted that way.

“You clearly do not care” combines an observation with an assumption about motive. A more useful message can still say that you felt hurt, ignored, or unimportant while leaving room for information you may not have.

03
Make the next step visible.

If your real goal is clarification, an apology, a change in behavior, a boundary, or a concrete answer, say that directly. A recipient can respond more constructively when the message gives them a clear path forward.

Research behind the pause

Brief delays and slower breathing can reduce the intensity of an immediate response.

A 2024 experimental study of romantic couples found that brief forced breaks of 5, 10, or 15 seconds between provocation and the opportunity to respond reduced negative emotion and aggressive responding. Participants who voluntarily waited longer also showed lower negative emotion and less aggression. A systematic review of slow-breathing research also reported changes consistent with greater autonomic regulation, along with reductions in measures such as arousal, anxiety, anger, and confusion.

McCurry, May & Donaldson, 2024Communications Psychology. Experimental evidence on brief breaks during interpersonal conflict. Zaccaro et al., 2018Frontiers in Human Neuroscience. Systematic review of psychophysiological effects of slow breathing.

Process and research

How it works

BeforeUSend is a communication review tool. It helps users inspect wording before sending a message. The analysis provides indicators for reflection, not verified predictions about another person's reaction.

01

Set your goal

Enter a draft and select the purpose, recipient, and desired tone. These choices define what the review evaluates.

02

Review language patterns

Immediate JavaScript checks identify phrases linked to blame, pressure, hostility, direct requests, and boundaries. Punctuation and emphasis add context.

03

Interpret the results

Escalation potential and goal alignment are heuristic indicators. Highlighted phrases show where wording may warrant a second look.

04

Consider a revision

Use an immediate wording revision and compare it against the original. You retain final control over the message.

Research informing the experience

Research on conflict regulation suggests that a short pause before responding can reduce negative emotion and aggressive reactions in studied settings. Reviews of slow breathing report associations with physiological regulation and reduced arousal. These findings inform the pause and reflection exercises, but they do not validate the accuracy of BeforeUSend's NLP scores.

Under the hood

Methodology and privacy

BeforeUSend provides immediate language-pattern analysis and an optional instant wording revision to help you review a message before sending it. It looks for how language might land, not what the sender secretly feels or what a recipient will definitely do.

01 / LANGUAGE ANALYSIS

Beyond detecting keywords

Natural Language Processing (NLP) lets software analyze patterns in human language. The immediate analysis uses transparent JavaScript language rules to identify wording patterns and compare them with the selected goal. The revision tool uses immediate, transparent phrase-based edits.

That distinction matters: “I need you to stop contacting me” may be a healthy boundary, while “Fine. Do whatever you want” could be dismissive in a tense exchange. Neither phrase has one guaranteed interpretation.

02 / THE RULES

Making feedback explainable

JavaScript rules separately check observable cues such as direct threats, insults, blanket statements (“always,” “never”), accusatory questions, ultimatums, repeated punctuation, and capitalization. These signals help identify which exact wording to revisit.

Rules alone can overreact to harmless phrases or miss subtle meanings. The immediate results highlight detected expressions so readers can assess them directly. These rules are limited and may miss context, sarcasm, or implicit meaning. Highlighting is a prompt to reflect, not proof of hostility.

03 / THE OUTPUTS

What the scores actually mean

Escalation potential is an estimate of how strongly the wording might increase tension or defensiveness. Goal alignment estimates whether the wording supports the selected goal, such as apologizing, asking a question, resolving a misunderstanding, or setting a boundary.

Observable language signals summarize patterns such as politeness, frustration, and defensiveness. Signal clarity describes how clear the detected signals appear; it does not measure the probability that an interpretation is correct.

All 0 to 100 scores are experimental heuristics, not empirically calibrated probabilities or clinically validated measurements.

04 / THE REWRITE

Same intent, different delivery

The rewriting feature adjusts wording to make messages clearer, calmer, and more professional. Compare the suggestion with your original draft to confirm it communicates your intended meaning.

Generated suggestions can miss nuance, change meaning, or overlook essential context. Always review the revision before copying or sending it.

PRIVACY BY DESIGN

Your message stays in your browser

Immediate message analysis and optional rewriting run in the browser; this deployment does not provide a server endpoint for receiving drafts, and it does not intentionally save message text to a database.
