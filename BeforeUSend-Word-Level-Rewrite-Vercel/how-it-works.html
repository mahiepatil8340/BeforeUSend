Analysis flow

From draft to review

BeforeUSend reviews the message, compares its wording with common communication patterns, and returns immediate estimates with clear evidence.

Natural language processing

What the NLP compares

Your draft
  • Specific wording
  • Requests and boundaries
  • Blame and criticism
  • Capitalization and punctuation
compares with
General language patterns
  • Common communication phrasing
  • Curated escalation phrases
  • Polite and cooperative wording
  • Known conflict cues
  • The instant analyzer compares a draft against built-in phrase patterns and communication rules.
  • It does not query or compare the draft against a live general-language corpus.
  • Its numeric scores are heuristic estimates rather than validated probabilities.

Three additional NLP research sources

Research informing language analysis

01

Sentiment classification

  • Pang, Lee, and Vaithyanathan (2002) investigated how machine learning can classify the sentiment of written language.
  • The study illustrates why sentiment is more complex than counting positive and negative terms.
Pang et al. (2002)
02

Contextual word representations

  • Devlin and colleagues (2019) introduced BERT, which considers words in relation to the surrounding text.
  • This research explains why context-aware language models can interpret a phrase differently across sentences.
Devlin et al. (2019)
03

Pretraining and model quality

  • Liu and colleagues (2019) showed that training choices influence language-model performance.
  • This work helps motivate careful benchmarking instead of assuming that a larger model guarantees reliable interpretation.
Liu et al. (2019)

These studies describe foundational NLP methods. The current instant BeforeUSend analyzer uses built-in linguistic rules and does not run BERT, RoBERTa, or a live corpus comparison. These publications do not establish the accuracy of its scores.
